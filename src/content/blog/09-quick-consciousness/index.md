#

_Posted on April 29, 2018 by thelaboriousgame_

I want to make a quick pass at an argument to motivate thinking that the physical and consciousness are distinct, namely with the conceivability argument. This argument is by no means original or uncommon these days, but I’ll briefly rehearse it anyhow. One way of putting the conceivability argument for mind-body–or more specifically here, consciousness-brain–dualism might follow as such:

1. The opposite of any logically contingent state of affairs is possible. (Axiom)
2. That brain states bear consciousness is logically contingent. (Premise)
3. Therefore, it’s possible that brain states might be unconscious. That is, brain states could exist without consciousness. (1, 2)
4. For any _x_, and for any _y_, if _x_ can exist without _y_, _x_ and _y_ are distinct. (Axiom)
5. Therefore, brain states and consciousness are distinct. (3, 4)

For the sake of clarity I should say that by _logically contingent_ I mean something to the effect of “_x_ is logically contingent if and only if it’s possible that _x_ and it’s possible that not-_x_,” and possible meaning that the concept of _x_ contains no logical contradiction (as, say, the concept of something that’s bigger than itself would). So here we’re working with maximally abstract notions of possibility and contingency.

Now, propositions 1 and 4, as I’ve indicated, seem to me to be indubitably true axioms. The rest, granting proposition 2, follows logically. So the questions is, do we grant it? This is the rub, because one might object that mere logical possibility is not enough to guarantee actual possibility (that is, _metaphysical_ possbility). Much ink has been spilt on this, of course. It may in the end be undecidedable (though I can’t say). Still, the above argument does, on the the face of it, give some motivation for thinking that consciousness and the brain are not the one and the same, even if intimately related or inseparable.

What does _that_ mean, exactly? Tough to say. The problems of substance dualism are well known, of course. I myself am partial to some form of panpsychism.
